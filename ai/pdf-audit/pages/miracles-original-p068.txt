On November 16, 1985, a Saturday, I
stood almost all day dipping candles, and I
could hardly move because I was so tight and
stiff and in so much pain. We were to have a
special prayer service that night for St. Her-
man of Alaska and I wanted to go. After
cleaning up from candlemaking I went to our
room (my husband's and mine, but he was
away) and lay on the floor for a few minutes,
which usually helped the pain and stiffness,
so I could go to prayers. I was still in a lot
of pain and was stiff, but I decided to force
myself to the chapel anyway. So I limped ov-
er; but after climbing the stairs, I just could
not bring myself to climb the next flight and
stay in the chapel for an hour or so. There
were not many people there, which influenced
my decision because I would not have been
able to hide my discomfort with only a few
people in the chapel. So I asked for forgive-
ness and thought that at least I could read
about St. Herman in my room during the
prayer service, and in that way would not be
turning my back on him completely.

St. Herman and figured there would be some-
thing about him in the early issues of Ortho-
dox America that we have. So I took the first
several years worth of copies to my room to
read. There was not anything about St. Her-
man in the issues I had, but there was a
great deal about Blessed John Maximovitch;
