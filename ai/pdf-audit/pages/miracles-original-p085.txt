Once while I was in his office they
brought him his dinner: I remember that it
was a bowl of borsht and a cup of kisel [fruit
pudding}. He was alone and I was in the ad-
jacent room, where they also brought to me
this modest meal. And through the open door
I clearly saw how Blessed John poured the
sweet kisel into the bowl of borsht and began
to eat this untasty mixture. At that time such
things seemed to me, yet a child, absolutely
natural for the bishop to do.

All the children and altar boys loved
the bishop, in spite of his strictness. (Once
the bishop ordered the warden Michael to
whip some guilty ones for their mischief.)
